Visual direction

Training route atlas

My Workout Pal treats a program as a route that adapts to available equipment without redrawing completed ground. The cultural source is a field atlas: contour notation, waypoints, route alternatives, elevation summaries, and compact trip sheets. The interface uses that grammar to explain program structure and state, not to decorate an ordinary fitness dashboard.

Grounded directions

The seven grounded directions were ordered by audience resonance and product clarity:

  1. A coach's laminated session card and clipboard grid.
  2. A track split ledger and timing board.
  3. A topographic route atlas with waypoints and elevation paths.
  4. Industrial equipment calibration plates and inspection stamps.
  5. A Swiss sports broadside and training notice system.
  6. A transit departure board and wayfinding network.
  7. A field notebook with indexed tabs and ruled observations.

The Impeccable direction roll assigned the third candidate with seed ba529732.

Challenger review

  • Live ASCII scene: declined. It identifies computation more than training and makes dense workout input less clear. The atlas keeps its discipline of one coherent rendering grid.
  • Film cutting bench: competitive on task clarity. A persisted stop flag and reachable deferred items fit workout resume and skipped movements, but the film vocabulary weakens fitness identification. The atlas adopts its decisive visible state marks.
  • Alphabet storm: declined. Semantic weather does not identify the audience or improve logging clarity. The atlas keeps its principle that motion must express a state change.
  • Industrial quote grammar: competitive on audience identification. Training users recognize equipment, labels, and hard state, but hazard treatment is too aggressive for recovery and settings. The atlas adopts literal status stamps and strong contrast.
  • Character goods catalog: declined. It can make a library approachable but weakens the precision and ownership required for workout data. The atlas keeps only a small focus response for selected waypoints.
  • Racing league: competitive on performance identity. It signals progression but overstates speed and competition. The atlas adopts oversized legible waypoint numerals and directional motion.

World

  • Ground: Warm mineral paper with restrained grain and contour notation.
  • Ink: Deep blue-green for navigation, rules, and core text.
  • Active route: Coral for selection and forward action.
  • Compatible state: Lichen green for equipment compatibility and confirmed preservation.
  • Secondary marks: Stone gray for contours, inactive routes, and supporting data.
  • Type: A compact condensed display face for route numerals and short labels, paired with a calm humanist sans serif for reading and data entry.
  • Components: Waypoints, ruled route sheets, stamped status labels, contour dividers, and path-linked progress. Avoid generic floating card grids.

Cross-surface behavior

  • The program overview maps five days as route waypoints.
  • Equipment changes reroute only substituted waypoints and show preserved or cleared details.
  • The runner advances a visible route and stamps operations as pending, saved, or failed.
  • History freezes completed routes instead of applying the active map.
  • Analytics use elevation and traverse graphics only when they improve comprehension and always include a table.
  • Settings and authentication use quiet field sheets with the same rules and stamps, not a new visual metaphor.

Signature interaction

Switching equipment profiles redraws only affected path segments and moves substituted waypoint labels. A confirmation sheet lists the same changes in text. Reduced-motion mode replaces path drawing with an immediate route swap and a concise change summary.

Honest risk

Cartographic material can become decorative and reduce information density. Each surface must keep the user task, labels, controls, and status readable without the contours. No route motif can replace semantic structure or honest save feedback.
