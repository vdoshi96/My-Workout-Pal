# Authenticated program customization and insights plan

## User outcome

A verified member can turn either five-day starter into a genuinely personal program without weakening the immutable-history model. The member can create, clone, activate, and rename owned programs; create a compatible private movement; add, replace, remove, and reorder exercises; add, rename, reorder, or remove non-core day sections while retaining a populated core section; edit sets, ranges, targets, notes, rest, day labels, and cardio templates; preview and confirm an equipment change; change presentation preferences; and then see personal records and progress derived from completed owned workout data.

The flow must prove that customization affects only the active published revision. A completed workout keeps its original exercise, equipment, measurement kind, canonical values, prescribed targets, note, and cardio meaning after later program edits or a dumbbell-to-barbell change. A later preference may convert only presentation labels and rounded display values; it never reinterprets the original input or stored kilograms, metres, seconds, and seconds per kilometre. Guests retain the complete public read-only plan and exercise resources. Sign-in remains necessary only for owned customization and persisted training data.

This checkpoint extends the credential-free authenticated fixture. It does not claim literal offline behavior, post-load authentication expiry, multi-tab conflict resolution, account deletion, hosted Firebase, email delivery, Google consent, secure production cookies, authenticated production playback, or Vercel Spend Management. Those remain separately planned evidence lanes.

## Navigation

The production information architecture remains unchanged. The fixture adds only mirrors of real private routes beneath `tests/fixtures/authenticated-app`; no harness route enters `src/app`.

1. Verified synthetic Alice opens `/app`, creates or reuses a dumbbell starter, and establishes one completed Pull workout through the already-proven runner boundary.
2. Alice opens the program collection at `/app/programs`, creates a separate barbell starter and verifies its complete five-day structure, clones an owned program, and then explicitly reactivates the original dumbbell program. Exactly one program is active after every successful operation, and the later equipment preview therefore cannot be a no-op.
3. Alice opens `/app/library/custom/new`, creates a private weight-and-repetitions movement compatible with dumbbells and the barbell-enabled profile, and returns through the private library to the program editor.
4. At `/app/program/edit`, Alice renames a day and section, explicitly reviews and removes Push's non-core Accessory section, re-adds Accessory, adds and reorders the private movement, changes its sets, range, target, rest, and notes, changes cardio values, and publishes one immutable revision. The populated Core section remains visible and cannot be removed throughout the sequence.
5. With the edited dumbbell program still active, Alice returns to `/app/program/edit` and uses the editor's full Equipment Profile control to open a real dumbbell-to-barbell preview. A dirty draft may open and cancel the preview without losing any local value, but cannot confirm because equipment confirmation is based only on the active published revision. After the draft is published or discarded, Alice reviews every canonical substitution and custom-exercise compatibility result and confirms the barbell-enabled revision from the editor. The saved equipment revision becomes the editor's new clean baseline.
6. Alice opens the historical Pull route and verifies that the saved dumbbell workout is unchanged. She then opens `/app/settings`, changes units, time zone, and reduced-motion preference, and confirms that storage meaning did not change.
7. Alice completes a qualifying owned workout, opens `/app/prs`, and sees persisted personal-record projection rows. `/app/progress` presents totals and the daily series derived from completed set and cardio logs in the selected units and time zone.
8. Synthetic Bob attempts Alice's opaque program, custom-exercise, workout/history, and projection sources. Foreign and missing private resources remain indistinguishable, while Bob's valid empty progress surface contains none of Alice's values.

Browser navigation uses the visible application links and keyboard activation for material actions. Direct fixture API requests are limited to exact ownership, idempotency, cache, and database-effect assertions that cannot be observed safely through page copy alone.

## Domain types and invariants

The existing `ProgramPublishInput` remains the only publish envelope. It keeps exactly the five canonical day keys in order, one walker and one runner template per day, bounded names and notes, and exactly one catalog or owner-custom identity per prescription. The client never supplies owner identity, publication status, revision number, logging kind, equipment truth, or a persistence outcome.

Section editing receives pure helpers before UI work. A day may contain each of `strength`, `accessory`, and `core` at most once and must always retain exactly one nonempty `core` section. Core is a mandatory, non-removable section kind; an owned revision may rename its bounded title but may not remove it, duplicate it, or publish it empty. A section has a stable draft key, a bounded nonblank title, a position derived from array order, and at least one prescription at publication. Adding is limited to a missing non-core kind; moving normalizes section and prescription order without mutating the input; renaming trims only at schema validation; and removing a nonempty non-core section requires an explicit review that names the exercises removed from the unpublished draft. Adding then cancelling exercise selection leaves an honest invalid empty non-core section until it is populated or removed. Both owned equipment profiles therefore remain complete five-day programs with core plus walker and runner cardio on every day, even when strength or accessory composition changes.

Program collection invariants remain at most 24 roots per owner, exactly one active root after onboarding, a complete five-day published revision per active root, and owner-scoped clone sources. A clone copies the complete revision graph using new opaque row IDs and preserves catalog or same-owner custom references without sharing mutable descendants.

A custom exercise has one owner, a unique server-generated owner-scoped key, a bounded display name, one logging kind, one or more required equipment IDs, original instructions, bounded aliases, and zero-to-two normalized unique YouTube IDs. Its logging kind cannot reinterpret a referenced program, workout snapshot, personal record, or progress row. Equipment compatibility is resolved from server rows, never from a client label.

Equipment confirmation accepts the active program ID, exact base revision ID, target profile, and retry-stable idempotency key. The server recomputes the equipment-change preview. Canonical substitutions retain sets, compatible ranges, rest, section, order, and notes; movement-specific targets clear when meaning is incompatible. Compatible custom movements remain referenced. Any incompatible custom movement blocks confirmation. The operation creates one active revision and never rewrites another program or an immutable workout snapshot.

Program targets remain canonical kilograms, metres, seconds, and seconds per kilometre in the draft/publish contract. Canonical distance accepts the database's three-decimal metre scale, so sub-mile imperial targets do not fail an integer-only schema. The trusted publish schema rejects excess canonical precision before positivity and shape checks—`160.934 m` is valid while `0.0001 m` cannot round into an invalid stored zero; cardio may explicitly be zero but may not exceed the canonical scale. The editor receives the server-derived unit preference, converts canonical values to labelled `kg`/metres/seconds-per-kilometre or `lb`/miles/seconds-per-mile presentation, and performs strict finite display-to-canonical conversion and domain rounding before publication. Changing units never reinterprets the number already stored in a canonical revision, and fixture adapters reuse this production boundary rather than copying conversion rules.

Personal-record projection is a versioned deterministic transformation executed inside the successful `complete_session` transaction. It considers only work sets belonging to exercises marked completed and uses the effective catalog or custom exercise identity after a valid substitution. Warm-up sets and skipped exercises never produce candidates. Candidate metrics are:

- weight and repetitions: maximum weight, repetitions, per-set volume, and Epley estimated one-repetition maximum only for a positive load; a valid zero-load set does not invent a zero estimated maximum;
- bodyweight repetitions: repetitions;
- duration: duration;
- distance and duration: distance and duration.

Each emitted candidate is finite and rounded to the database's canonical scale, links to its exact owner-scoped source set, records one calculation version and achieved timestamp, and uses the stable owner/exercise-kind/exercise/type/source identity. Positive repetitions may emit `max_repetitions` even when external load is zero; `max_weight` and `volume` are emitted only when their rounded canonical values are strictly positive. Estimated one-repetition maximum is emitted only for positive load and uses `weightKg × (1 + repetitions / 30)`, rounded to three decimals; one repetition is not special-cased. Thus a valid zero-load weight-and-repetitions set emits repetitions only, never zero weight, zero volume, or zero Epley records. A recognized newer calculation version upserts that source candidate's canonical value and version while preserving source identity and achieved time; it never downgrades an unknown or newer stored version. Versions may correct computation only while metric meaning, canonical unit, and scale remain comparable. A semantic metric change requires a new record type rather than a version bump.

A versioned rebuild replays the same transformation over already-completed owned sessions, including sessions whose completion operation already has a terminal idempotency receipt. It is safe to resume and rerun, reports proposed/applied counts, and requires an explicit apply mode before changing database rows; it never edits immutable source logs. During a resumable version upgrade, mixed recognized versions across processed and unprocessed sources are temporarily comparable. For each processed source, the current algorithm upserts emitted metrics and removes older recognized metrics it no longer emits, so removed current-version metrics cannot survive as stale older-version winners. Unknown newer rows are never deleted or downgraded, but the current read model excludes them because this build cannot attest to their computation.

The read model resolves the exercise label from the immutable owner-scoped workout exercise state or snapshot reached through each winning source set, never from the movement's current catalog/custom name. It accepts a catalog candidate only when its catalog ID equals the source state's effective catalog ID and both custom IDs are null; the symmetric rule applies to custom candidates. Identity-mismatched rows fail closed rather than borrowing a snapshot label. Its group key includes `catalog` or `custom` identity kind because the two tables may legally contain the same UUID. A database-side aggregate computes the global maximum per identity kind/exercise/metric and selects the newest 500 winning groups. For each selected group, SQL computes the complete exact-tie count but returns only the newest 20 source links plus `hasMoreSources`; the UI never implies that the bounded link sample is the total. The application never materializes the owner's entire candidate history, and an oldest-first pre-group row cap is forbidden because it can discard newer winners and ties. Replaying completion or the rebuild cannot duplicate a current-version candidate.

Progress remains a read projection from persisted terminal sessions, work-set rows, cardio rows, and owner preferences. All-time totals and abandoned count use bounded database aggregates. The daily timeline is limited deterministically to the newest 180 completed workouts and exposes its included count/limit/truncated state; the UI labels that scope rather than implying complete history when truncated. Within the timeline, logs and cardio are indexed by session once so derivation remains linear. All set-derived metrics use work sets only: warm-ups contribute to history but not volume, duration, distance, or Epley values. `weight_reps` volume is `weightKg × repetitions`; `bodyweight_reps` volume is only `addedWeightKg × repetitions` because body mass is not available; duration and distance logging kinds contribute zero volume. Cardio rows from completed sessions continue to contribute time and distance, so the combined UI total is labelled `Logged distance` rather than `Cardio distance`. Abandoned or resumable sessions do not inflate totals. Weight is canonical kilograms, distance canonical meters, duration integer seconds, and the selected unit system and IANA time zone affect presentation and grouping only. Existing optional summary rows report projection metadata but never override contradictory source logs.

## Persistence contracts

The isolated fixture applies the real migrations and deterministic starter/video seed to one in-memory PGlite database per test scope. It creates only synthetic Alice and Bob profiles. Fixture route adapters invoke production request schemas, CSRF checks, repositories, read models, and client components. They do not connect to Neon, copy SQL business rules, or hard-code successful response bodies.

Program create, clone, activation, publication, and equipment confirmation remain owner-scoped atomic transactions with the existing row locks, stale-revision checks, and retry-stable idempotency receipts. A stale base or expected-active revision rolls back without changing the active pointer. Custom exercise creation reserves its owner-scoped operation key and writes exercise, equipment, aliases, and videos atomically. Preference updates use optimistic `expectedUpdatedAt` and preserve canonical workout rows.

Every program-root or revision mutation response distinguishes the program/revision affected by the original idempotent operation from the program/revision that is active when the response is read, and identifies a replay. Replaying a create, clone, activation, publication, or equipment change after another activation never reactivates the earlier target. Client copy and navigation use those explicit identities: a matching target may become the visible baseline/overview, while a stored-but-no-longer-active target remains in collection context and is never mislabeled as current.

Section title and order persist in new immutable program-section rows created by publication. Removing or reordering an unpublished section mutates only browser draft state until a successful publish. Earlier revisions, sessions, exercise snapshots, set logs, cardio logs, personal-record candidates, and progress sources remain byte-stable.

Personal-record candidates are inserted in the same transaction that marks the workout completed. A late projection failure rolls back both the projection and terminal session state rather than leaving a completed workout with partial records. The existing operation idempotency receipt returns the original terminal meaning on replay. The versioned rebuild scans already-completed owner-scoped sessions in deterministic committed batches, invokes the identical projection rules, records its version/progress durably, and can resume after interruption or rerun to a no-op. Dry-run also advances a local cursor through short read transactions rather than holding one database transaction across all history. Its operator command is dry-run by default and requires `--apply`; the repository-only interruption hook is never exposed by the CLI. Its durable cursor is only the globally unique workout-session UUID, is cleared on completion, and never stores a Firebase UID, so deleting an account cannot strand an external owner key in maintenance state. Progress queries remain read-only and owner filtered.

All production pages derive the viewer from the revocation-aware HTTP-only Firebase session. Every permanent mutation requires a verified identity. The credential-free fixture begins with one immutable synthetic viewer selected by the loopback runner's fixture-only request header before navigation; production source contains no such selector. Request bodies, paths, query parameters, local storage, and screenshots never carry a selectable UID.

Private mutations preserve the production order: same-origin double-submit CSRF, authenticated and mutation-eligible viewer, bounded body, strict schema, then database construction and owner-scoped repository work. Every private response, including authentication, validation, foreign, missing, conflict, and injected failure outcomes, contains `Cache-Control: private, no-store` or an equivalently strict no-store directive.

Alice and Bob have distinct fixed owners. Bob's foreign program, revision, custom exercise, workout, history source, set source, and personal-record source must match a random missing identifier in status, stable code, normalized body, cache headers, and database effect. For rendered Next 404 documents, normalization replaces only the caller-supplied opaque resource ID and preserves the rest of the document for equality; neither response may contain an owner UID or the other opaque ID. Bob's ordinary progress page is a valid owner-scoped empty read, not a 404; it must contain zero Alice identifiers, values, names, notes, or counts. Unverified Alice may inspect private read models but cannot create, publish, confirm equipment, save preferences, or complete a workout.

Hosted Firebase registration, password verification/recovery, Google consent, cookie exchange, post-load expiry, revocation, and recent-auth deletion are not simulated by this lane.

## Loading, empty, error, interrupted, and worst-case behavior

Server Components use the existing private loading and error boundaries. Missing profile or active program returns to onboarding. Corrupt program graphs, zero or multiple active rows, missing source records, and projection rows whose source is foreign or absent fail closed; the UI never invents a replacement active program or sample statistic.

Every client mutation keeps a single idempotency key across slow, failed, malformed, and ambiguously interrupted responses. Controls are disabled only while their own operation is pending. A successful repository write followed by a bounded injected `500` is reconciled by replay or fresh server render without creating a second root, revision, custom exercise, preference update, or personal-record candidate. A response that fails structural parsing never advances local state.

An accepted program create/clone/activation/publication/equipment response may be replayed after another tab or request activates a different program. That replay returns both affected and current identities. It reports the earlier operation as stored, does not reset an editor to a different root, does not call an inactive root active, and does not navigate to an overview under false copy.

A stale editor or equipment preview preserves the visible draft/review and asks for reload; it never silently rebases. A custom exercise deleted or made incompatible before publication returns the same safe result as unavailable input and leaves the active revision unchanged. A late database failure during clone, publish, equipment change, preference update, completion, or personal-record projection rolls back the whole transaction.

The worst path for this lane is: a completed historical dumbbell workout exists; Alice edits and publishes an active program; another accepted request advances the base revision while the response is lost; Alice attempts a stale equipment confirmation; and Bob probes the historical source. Recovery must show the server-confirmed current revision exactly once, reject the stale confirmation, preserve the old workout snapshot, and disclose nothing to Bob.

Literal offline events, page-close recovery, another-tab writes, and post-load auth expiry are deliberately not claimed here because they require the separate interruption/conflict lane.

## Acceptance criteria

- Both dumbbell-only and barbell-enabled owned starters remain complete five-day programs with core and walker/runner defaults before customization.

The journey explicitly reactivates the original dumbbell root before proving a non-no-op dumbbell-to-barbell confirmation; the separate barbell starter remains structurally valid.

- A verified member can create one compatible custom exercise and use it in the active program; a foreign, missing, or incompatible custom exercise cannot be published.
- The editor can add, rename, reorder, and remove non-core sections; add, replace, reorder, and remove exercises; and edit day names, sets, ranges, targets, rest, notes, and cardio. Every day retains one populated non-removable core section, metric/imperial inputs convert to canonical kg/metres/seconds-per-kilometre, and publication creates one immutable revision.

- Equipment-change preview lists exact canonical substitutions, retains compatible custom exercises, visibly names incompatible custom movements and required equipment, recomputes on the server, and requires explicit confirmation. A blocked/cancelled preview creates no revision or profile change.
- An immutable workout snapshot recorded before editing and equipment confirmation retains the same canonical exercise names, targets, kilograms, meters, seconds, set/cardio values, and notes afterward. Before a preference change, the rendered display is identical; after a preference change, only presentation units and rounding may convert while raw canonical values and snapshot identity remain unchanged.
- Completing a workout transactionally creates deterministic, idempotent personal-record projection candidates from eligible work sets. Warm-ups, skipped exercises, foreign sources, invalid values, and replay do not create records.
- Personal records preserve exact tie accounting, expose at most the newest 20 owned source links with a truthful total/has-more state, use immutable source-workout labels, and cannot lose a newer winner to a pre-group query limit. The versioned rebuild idempotently projects existing completed sessions and terminal completion receipts, upgrades surviving recognized source metrics, and removes stale older recognized metrics no longer emitted. Progress all-time totals and the bounded newest-180-workout timeline use the defined weight/bodyweight added-load volume formulas, derive only from persisted completed owned work sets plus cardio logs, exclude warm-ups/interrupted/abandoned sessions, and contain no sample values.
- Preference changes alter display units, selected time-zone presentation, and motion behavior only; canonical kilograms, meters, seconds, historical snapshots, and record values do not change. This browser journey proves the alternate time zone persists; it does not claim a local-day regrouping unless fixture timestamps actually cross that zone boundary.
- Alice/Bob foreign and missing private-resource outcomes are indistinguishable, no-store, and side-effect equivalent. Bob's valid empty insights disclose none of Alice's data.
- Chromium and WebKit each complete phone/tablet/desktop scoped geometry checks, plus the journey's keyboard, serious/critical Axe, reduced-motion, dark-mode, forced-color where supported, console, page-error, first-party request-failure, HTTP-failure, and overflow collectors clean except for explicitly consumed failure scenarios. Actual 200 percent browser zoom is a distinct manual pre-merge acceptance check, with any engine limitation reported truthfully.
- The fixture remains credential-free and absent from the production route manifest. No provider, Neon, Vercel, spend, or production state changes in this implementation lane.
